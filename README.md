# Safe Streets Addis

**A privacy-first, AI-assisted road safety enforcement design for Addis Ababa and other African cities. Road safety as preventive medicine.**

> Citizen proposal, draft for discussion (v0.3, 20 November 2026). A citizen contribution offered openly for comment.

About 400 people die on Addis Ababa's roads every year by police count, and 86 percent of them are pedestrians. Speeding contributed to nearly half of fatal crashes in 2023/24. Safe Streets Addis shows how a city can use what it already owns (traffic cameras, the new RFID/QR number plates, digital fine payment, and the electronic enforcement allowed by Ethiopia's Regulation 557/2024) to enforce speed and red-light rules fairly:

- **Every vehicle.** Cameras at paired roadside sites read every number plate (private cars, minibuses, buses, trucks, motorcycles and government vehicles) to measure average speed and detect red-light and lane offences. Public transport and fleets get one extra layer: speed and tamper reporting, starting with a free coaching app.
- **AI on existing cameras.** A small computer at each camera turns video into coded movement data that is small enough for the 2G network. Video is fetched only for flagged cases.
- **A person checks every case** before any notice is sent. Warnings come before fines, appeals are easy, and refunds are given if the system is wrong.
- **Speed limits are set centrally** for each camera location: lower near schools, hospitals and markets, and changeable for roadworks.
- **Privacy by design.** Only drivers with a confirmed offence are looked up; every other vehicle's records are deleted within hours. The data is used for one purpose only, and identity is held by a separate body.
- **Everything is documented** in a signed audit trail, so that any later change can be detected.
- **It can start lean:** a warning-only pilot on one corridor needs an estimated USD 27,000 to 125,000 in cash.
- **Expected impact once city-wide (central estimate):** about 40 deaths and about 190 serious injuries avoided each year. These are modelled estimates.
